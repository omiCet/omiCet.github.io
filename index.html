Research

Disequilibrium Retrieval for WASP-39b
The 2023 detection of photochemically produced sulfur dioxide in the atmosphere of the hot Jupiter WASP-39b revealed that the effects of disequilibrium processes on the compositions of exoplanets can be significant. Failing to account for such processes in atmospheric retrievals can lead to biased estimates of the carbon-to-oxygen ratio and metallicity, which are key to understanding planet formation. Using chemical kinetics modeling, I am working on incorporating disequilibrium retrievals into the open-source retrieval code POSEIDON, and benchmarking this technique on the full JWST dataset for WASP-39b.

This research is supported by the Rawlings Cornell Presidential Research Scholar program, and for the summer of 2025 was supported by the Roger and Mary Lou West Undergraduate Research Fellowship. My excellent advisors for this work are Dr. Nikole Lewis, Dr. Ryan Challener, and Elijah Mullens.

Reanalysis of the NIRSpec PRISM Spectrum of a Sub-Neptune Exoplanet
Sub-Neptune exoplanets are both common and highly mysterious. During my CASSUM fellowship at Chalmers University (working with Dr. Jonathan Tan, Dr. Giuseppe Morello, and Gareb Fernández Rodríguez), I analyzed the transmission spectrum of the sub-Neptune LP 791-18c via a suite of retrievals, seeking to better understand degeneracies in the interpretation of its JWST transmission spectrum.
